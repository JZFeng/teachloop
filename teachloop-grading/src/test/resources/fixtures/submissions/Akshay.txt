I disagree with the notion that anyone who speaks a language can teach that language to someone else. Teaching is more than just having knowledge of a language and how it works. As the module emphasizes, there is a technique to teaching, and that it must be equally balanced with language content to resonate with students. A person might be good at speaking a language, but may not be aware of all the several nuances in teaching that language.

One of the most important things about teaching is to foster curiosity. By presenting information in the zone of proximal development (or i+1), a learner can become inquisitive and feel empowered to pick up that information, since it's at a level they can comprehend, and is possible for them to eventually learn and understand.

Teachers are also aware of their body language, or teacher talk, which further helps their students resonate with the concepts and languages that are being taught. Techniques like i+1 or body language make it clear that teaching is an elaborate process, and it supports my argument that teaching is more than just being able to speak the language.
