My name is Alejandro D. Alvarez and I am personally opposed to the assumption that anyone who speaks the language can teach the language. I believe that teaching the language requires more than mere speaking it. There is a lot more to it than meets the surface, so to speak. Just like the metaphor of a cake in the previous video, it may be so yummy-looking yet this condition does not guarantee that anyone to whom the cake is offered will have a taste of it. Below are some of my views why I do not agree to this assumption.

Anyone who is attempting to teach the language needs to possess both theoretical knowledge and technical skills. A teacher needs to understand the underlying principles of how language is taught and learned. Therefore, before anyone can teach, one should take a thorough study and be carefully trained in this area. But this is not the end of it yet. After one has obtained the necessary knowledge, a teacher needs to test the knowledge by applying it into actual teaching. In this way, the teacher will discover the most useful techniques that help the students learn the language. Hence, anyone teaching the language needs to acquire knowledge about the language and develop a skill set.

Another equally important factor to be considered in teaching the language is the ability of the teacher to create or setup a learning environment that is inviting and motivating. This alludes to the metaphor of a cake in this lesson. A teacher should be able to capture the interest of the students in order to sustain their willingness to learn. Without this ingredient, the act of teaching the language will not produce an incremental result, or simply put it, it will not add any valuable input to students' existing knowledge. Therefore, skill to motivate is another tool that a language teacher should keep handy in order to successfully teach the language.

The last but not the least, I believe that a teacher should also need to be skillful in making the teaching process enjoyable and relevant which not everyone speaking the language possesses. Rather, possessing this kind of skill requires a teacher to be artistic, creative and resourceful. The teacher should be sensitive to feedback and reaction to have a grasp of the extent in which the students learn. From here, the teacher can design more effective teaching strategies or as the case may be. These summarize my views against the assumption that anyone who speaks the language can teach the language.
