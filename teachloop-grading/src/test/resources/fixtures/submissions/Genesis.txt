My name is Genesis, and I disagree with the statement that “anyone
who speaks a language can teach it.” Speaking a language and
teaching a language are two different skills. I remember that my
cousin’s husband could speak English very well, even though he was
not a native speaker. However, he did not know how to teach English
because he did not have knowledge about grammar, phonetics,
phonology, linguistics, or pronunciation.
This experience helped me understand that knowing a language does
not automatically mean knowing how to teach it. For example, Spanish
is my mother tongue, but if I had to teach Spanish, I would need
knowledge about how the language works and how to explain it to
other people. I may know how to use Spanish naturally, but I would not
necessarily know how to explain its phonemes, grammar, semantics, or
pronunciation.
I also believe that even having extensive knowledge of a language is
not enough to teach it effectively. Teaching requires pedagogy
because we need to understand how students learn, recognize their
different needs, and choose strategies that help them understand and
use the language. A person may know the language very well, but
without pedagogical knowledge, it can be difficult to transfer that
knowledge to students in a meaningful and effective way. For me,
being a language teacher means combining language knowledge
with pedagogical knowledge and an understanding of the learning
process.
