I disagree with the statement, "Anyone who speaks the language can teach the language." Speaking a language natively only means you have the content, but teaching requires technique and the ability to make meaning clear to learners.

Let me share a personal example. During my first year in the U.S., I lived with Adam, who is a native speaker, I had a really hard time understanding him because he spoke too fast, used a lot of unfamiliar idioms, and didn't adjust his pronunciation for a beginner. Looking back with what I've learned in this module, Adam lacked teaching technique. The biggest problem was that his input was at an i+10 level—far beyond my current understanding

In contrast, my company later hired Azul, who was an ESL teacher in Xi'an, China. She was an excellent teacher because she applied most core teaching techniques. She adjusted her Teacher Talk by speaking slowly and clearly, utilized rich body language and facial expressions to provide visual cues, and provided clear scaffolding step-by-step. Because she kept her instruction within my "Sweet Spot" , I was able to learn effectively and build confidence.

In conclusion, simply knowing a language doesn't make someone a teacher. Without masteries of Teacher Talk, scaffolding, and i+1 level control, even a native speaker cannot deliver effective learning experiences.
